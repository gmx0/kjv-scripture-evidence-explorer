Search algorithm 2.0

The implementation described here is versioned with the live workbench. Textual facts remain exact; evidentiary strength decays continuously instead of disappearing at arbitrary cutoffs.

CorpusKJV-PCE-BP-2010-v1Algorithm2.0.0
Versioning rule

Any change to formulas, component meanings, default weights, normalization, pruning, or tie-breaking requires a new algorithm version and a corresponding revision of this page.

Pipeline

  1. Preserve exact KJV text, case, offsets, and token position.
  2. Interpret the query as a reference, word, phrase, comparison, or modern topic.
  3. Generate separate candidate tiers from exact, phrase, morphological, letter-pattern, cross-reference, and topic-bridge evidence.
  4. Union and de-duplicate candidates.
  5. Calculate all applicable components and retain their explanations.
  6. Apply deterministic weights, rounding, and tie-breaking.

Total score

Score = 0.30J + 0.20P + 0.10R + 0.10O + 0.10K + 0.10M + 0.05L + 0.05C
SymbolWeightEvidence
J30%IDF-weighted shared vocabulary
P20%Exact phrases and ordered near-phrases
R10%Continuous rarity of shared words
O10%Preserved order and proximity
K10%Capitalization fidelity, discounted at sentence starts
M10%Lemmas, roots, compounds, and validated affixes
L5%Raw prefixes, suffixes, substrings, and shared letters
C5%Separately labeled editorial cross-references

Vocabulary and rarity

idf(t) = ln((N + 1) / (df(t) + 1)) + 1

Common words retain low nonzero weight. The system does not erase stop words because their order can distinguish constructions such as faith in, faith of, and justified by. Rarity is continuous rather than determined by a hard percentile.

Phrases and distance

gap decay = exp(−0.7 × intervening words)

Adjacent exact words receive the strongest phrase evidence. Ordered words separated by intervening text receive progressively less. A single aligned word receives only a small phrase/order contribution rather than being declared nonexistent.

Morphology and compounds

Relationship strength begins with the following values and is multiplied by source confidence, contextual compatibility, and component rarity:

RelationshipBase value
Inflectional lemma0.80
Root or derivational family0.65
Compound component0.50
Validated prefix and retained base0.20
Validated suffix and retained base0.15

Compound distance decays as exp(−0.5 × intervening words). Thus freewill can surface free, will, and a sibling such as selfwilled, while showing the path and never claiming that the forms are synonyms.

Raw letter evidence

Lpair = coverage³ × rarity × length decay × pattern multiplier

Prefixes and suffixes receive multiplier 1.00; internal substrings 0.75; ordered noncontiguous letters 0.25; unordered letters 0.10. One-letter overlap remains nonzero but receives length decay of about 0.001. Two letters receive about 0.010, three about 0.100, and four or more 1.000 before coverage and rarity reductions.

No double counting

For a word pair, the scorer uses its strongest established relationship: exact word, lemma, root, compound, validated affix, contiguous letters, ordered letters, then unordered letters. Weaker facts remain visible in the explanation but are not added again.

Candidate limits

Potential candidates are generated by tier and pruned deterministically using score upper bounds when necessary. The interface reports discovered, evaluated, and pruned counts. A pruned item means “not evaluated for display,” never “zero similarity.”

Stable ordering

Ties resolve by total score, phrase, rarity, capitalization, morphology, letter pattern, shared vocabulary, cross-reference evidence, and finally canonical Bible order.

Interpretive boundary

The algorithm measures visible relationships in a versioned corpus. Similarity is evidence for study, not proof that two passages have the same meaning. Dictionary bridges and editorial cross-references remain separate from Scripture-derived evidence.
